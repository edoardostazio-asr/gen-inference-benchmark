What we found

H3 Turbo vs H3 Turbo 2.4 s vs 8.1 s

On the like-for-like test, Reactor's first clip was watchable 2.4 s after the prompt and all six were ready at 13 s. MachGen's first arrived at 8.1 s and the sixth at 131 s.

Generation speed 2.1 s vs 2.8 s

Turbo against Turbo, Reactor generated each clip in 2.05–2.38 s including network; MachGen reported 2.76–2.89 s of GPU time. MachGen was faster only against Reactor's FastH3, a smaller distilled model.

MachGen queue Up to 67 s waiting

Queue waits appeared in every MachGen session, on two days and three models, and always in the queue, never the GPU. MachGen says its GPUs are overloaded at peak times.

Without the promo $0.05 → $0.10

H3 Turbo doubles to $0.10 per clip at list price. That is still 30–45% below Reactor FastH3 ($0.14–0.18) per clip. MachGen H3 has no promo and costs $0.20.

Reactor live models 50 fps · 1.6 s

LingBot World 2 streamed 1664×960 at 50 fps with no freezes and turned the camera 1.6 s after the command. The advertised "<50 ms" covers frame delivery only.

What the clips look like

Middle frame of each clip. Left to right: MachGen H3, MachGen H3 Turbo, Reactor FastH3. Rows use the same prompt and seed.

  • No obvious quality loss in either fast variant at this level of inspection.
  • MachGen H3 and Turbo hold the prompt's mood: the storm stays stormy, the rain stays rainy, the dawn light stays soft.
  • FastH3 is brighter and more saturated and sometimes drifts: the stormy lighthouse comes out sunny, the rainy balloon scene gains clear skies and extra balloons, and one balloon carries garbled text. Each provider runs its own prompt enhancer by default, which is the likely cause.
  • Single frames cannot show motion, temporal consistency or audio. A blind side-by-side of the clips is the next step.
Promo vs list price

What it costs once MachGen's promotion ends

MachGen bills per second of video: H3 Turbo is $0.02/s at list and $0.01/s during the current promotion; H3 is $0.04/s with no promotion. Reactor bills $0.035/s for every second a FastH3 session holds a GPU, whether it is building or playing.

Cost per 5.17 s clip, now and after the promotion

Only H3 Turbo is on promotion. Reactor's band runs from build time only (sessions kept busy building) to a continuous stream, where the session lasts as long as the video plays.

Method and limits

  • Small samples: 6 clips per MiniMax H3 variant, 15 and 12 FLUX images, one session per live model. Treat the numbers as indicative.
  • Every run came from one laptop in one time window. The network round trip to Reactor was 119–187 ms, which inflates every live figure. A run from a US-East cloud machine would tighten them.
  • MachGen's prices come from its billing API and pricebook (machgen.ai/pricing), Reactor's from its pricing API, both on 12 Oct 2026. The list-price scenarios apply MachGen's published non-promo rate to the same measured jobs.
  • MachGen states that Turbo latency "could be very high during promotion", so its queue times may improve once the promotion ends or with a sales contract.
  • The same seed gives different images on each provider, so quality was compared by eye on matched prompts, not scored.
  • MachGen refused its blocking (sync) endpoint for this account because it requires content moderation off, so it was measured on its queue API. No MachGen request was moved to partner GPUs.
  • Live latencies measure different actions per model: a prompt change (Helios), a camera command detected from motion between frames (LingBot), a marker square sent through the model and detected in its output (SANA), and a prompt to the first frame on screen (FastH3).
